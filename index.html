Leave the developer something they can use

Describe the moment

“Works fine” leaves the author guessing. “On a Pixel 6, Back closed the editor and my draft disappeared” gives them a device, a step and a result. Add the screenshot that shows where you got stuck.

Give an independent verdict

Try the feature yourself. Tell the author what surprised you, including what worked well. A partner owes you the same attention. A public review, a particular rating or a purchase is always a personal choice.

Close the loop

If a tester shows you a broken step, let them know when a fix is ready. Trying the same steps on the same device tells you whether the change helped. That second conversation is one reason developers stay after launch.

Community in numbers

When planning a test, start with who is testing now. When choosing a partner, look at their testing history. Those numbers answer different questions.

What the community has done

Open the public Telegram group and search for “PLATFORM SNAPSHOT” in the Daily Digest. Read the figures together. Each measures a different part of the work.

Production releases
Projects recorded as having reached production. This is a count of releases, so it cannot tell you the chance of approval on a first submission.
Check-ins over 30 days
Recent testing sessions recorded by participants. One tester can check in on several days.
Bugs reported
Problems participants have documented for authors to investigate in a particular build.
Improvements suggested
Suggestions sent to authors. An idea can start a discussion well before it becomes a shipped change.

Reach and current activity

The Mini App’s country breakdown shows how many people have tested in each country and how many are testing now. Compare those two counts when planning a language or regional test. A country’s past participation does not reserve testers for your next build.

Reliability

Check-in history shows how consistently someone follows a testing schedule. Missed days and early departures matter when you need a partner for two uninterrupted weeks.

Karma

A reputation balance shaped by platform events and recognition from other participants. An author can thank someone who helped them understand a difficult bug.

Contribution

Accepted reports and confirmed work earn contribution points. The sprint ranking shows recent activity. Lifetime contribution keeps the longer record.

Closed testing guide

What are Google Play’s current personal-account testing requirements?

For personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023, Google requires at least 12 testers opted in continuously for the previous 14 days before you can apply for production access. You also answer questions about testing and app readiness. Meeting the count alone does not grant automatic approval.

Google’s official testing requirements
Private testing or free mutual testing: what changes?

With free mutual testing in Telegram, you install other developers’ apps and complete daily check-ins to receive reciprocal testing. Private testing costs $20 with crypto or $23 with PayPal or bank transfer: we coordinate 12+ testers through a 15-day cycle, with no reciprocal tasks for you.

Why 15 days if Google asks for 14?

Our managed service includes an extra operational buffer day around counter updates and time-zone cutoffs in Play Console. It gives the cycle breathing room; it does not change Google’s 14-day minimum. If access is still closed after the cycle, we keep testing or refund the payment.

Real people and devices, or emulators and install farms?

Private testing uses real people on physical Android phones. We do not sell a counter of idle installs. Google may require more testing when engagement is insufficient. Real testing helps you find issues; your app must still meet Google Play policies, and no testing service can remove that responsibility.

Do I need to collect every tester’s email?

No. Add our Google Group as a tester group in Play Console: google-play-dev-test@googlegroups.com. Then enable all countries, send your changes for review and share the “Join on Android” testing link. The wizard includes screenshots for every setup step.

Can testers install my paid app without buying it?

Yes, after you configure License testing. In Play Console → Settings → License testing, choose Google Groups, add google-play-dev-test@googlegroups.com, keep RESPOND_NORMALLY and save. This is separate from adding the group to your closed testing track.

What if Google rejects my production access application?

If production access is not granted, we keep testing until it is, or we refund the payment. Fixes the app itself still needs, such as policy issues and broken flows, stay with you. Google makes the access decision. Our promise is that we do not stop at the first answer, and we do not keep the payment if we cannot get you there.

Do I have to give you access to my Play Console?

No passwords, admin invitations or console access are needed. You keep control of your developer account. Add the Google Group yourself and send the app name and closed testing link.

How do I pay and track the test?

Pay $20 by internal crypto exchange transfer, or $23 with PayPal or a bank transfer. For bank details, first create a requisites request and contact @garantXchange. Upload the payment receipt, then track your order in the website account. Telegram notifications require permission for the bot to message you. Detailed engagement is visible in your Play Console.

Why does Telegram sometimes ask for my phone number?

The Login Widget authorizes your browser, separately from the accounts installed in Telegram apps. A different browser, private tab or app clone may have a separate login session. If Telegram requests a number, enter the account you want on oauth.telegram.org and confirm in that Telegram account. DevTestHub never receives your phone number or login code.
